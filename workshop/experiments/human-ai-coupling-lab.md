# Human–AI Coupling Lab

**Status:** Experimental research program

The Human–AI Coupling Lab asks a narrow question:

> What is interaction history actually contributing to later behavior?

The lab does not exist to prove a preferred relational vocabulary.

It exists to measure recurring effects of pair history while preserving alternative explanations, negative cases, ambiguity, and failed hypotheses.

## Core research question

Does the interaction history of a particular human–AI pair materially change how later messages are interpreted, repaired, compressed, reconstructed, or connected?

A stricter controlled formulation is:

```
same facts + different history → different downstream behavior?
```

This is the **same destination, different road** test.

If two conditions contain the same final explicit propositions but acquire them through different developmental histories, and later behave differently on novel probes, that would be behavioral evidence for path dependence.

It would not, by itself, establish one unified mechanism.

## Alternative explanations that must remain live

- stored user facts
- immediate prompt content
- stylistic imitation
- generic personalization
- ordinary instruction-following
- common-ground effects

A result is not interesting because it fits the preferred story. It is interesting if it survives comparison with simpler explanations.

## Controlled conditions

### Condition A — Relational History

Information develops through misunderstanding, correction, repair, shorthand formation, and joint concept-building.

### Condition B — Fact Profile

The same final information is supplied directly as explicit facts.

### Condition C — Scrambled History

The same interactional material is present, but its developmental order is disrupted.

### Condition D — Alternate Partner

A comparable developmental history is created with a different fictional partner.

All conditions then receive the same probe prompts.

## Probe targets

- shorthand reconstruction
- ambiguity resolution
- correction propagation
- topology repair
- novel transfer
- false-partner transfer
- how much explicit information is required to reconstruct an intended meaning

The strongest counterfactual question is:

> Would the same current message reasonably produce the same interpretation if the relevant interaction history were removed?

## Research phases

1. **Naturalistic observation** — collect naturally occurring candidates before manufacturing examples.
2. **Coding** — classify events and identify recurring mechanisms.
3. **Counterfactual replay** — compare full history, reduced context, and naive/no-partner-history conditions where possible.
4. **Controlled replication** — construct controlled tests only after natural mechanisms emerge.
5. **Analysis** — compare ordinary memory, immediate-context inference, common-ground theory, and broader history-dependent coupling as explanations.

## Current methodological rule

Failures, rejected candidates, ambiguous events, correction history, and source lineage stay in the dataset.

The lab's working philosophy is:

> Don’t prove the forest exists. Measure the tracks.

## Mechanisms currently worth testing

- history-dependent reconstruction
- repair compression
- partner-specific shorthand
- context recovery
- topology repair
- convention revision
- emergent or adjacent bridge formation

No claim is made that all of these arise from one process.

## Research boundary

Conceptual language can be rich.

Research language must stay strict.

A recurring pattern earns investigation, not automatic promotion.

A tiny correction can reveal the topology of an active structure without proving why the compression works.

**Negative cases matter. Provenance matters. The road matters.**
